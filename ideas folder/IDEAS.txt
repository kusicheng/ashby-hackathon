AI Summary:

Dr. Parwani's view: the most useful applications of CONCH right now are context-based ones, where the model learns the language of pathology and acts as a guide: processing, classification, and helping pathologists think through cases. It is largely untested clinically and not ready for medical deployment. The barrier is not the model itself, which is scalable, but validation, safety, compliance, interpretability, and security. Getting from a research tool to a clinical application takes months of documented validation against external datasets and unknown variables. He stressed testing discipline: the right cases, the right controls, blind stress tests with unknown variables, consistency checks, and safety nets built into the program. For this hackathon he scoped what is feasible as safety and calibration of the model, plus external tools. He also pointed to education uses: guiding trainees like a hint system, since experienced pathologists need knowledge support rather than diagnostic help, and using foundation models to reduce subjectivity and variability in diagnosis.

Dr. Raj Dash:
AI Summary:
Dr. Dash (Duke, Vice Chair of Anatomic Pathology and Informatics) answered in writing instead of a call. His central point: trust is the wrong frame. What matters is transparency and verifiability, with a human in the loop who can evaluate what the AI produced, because a doctor should never blindly trust an AI. For retrieval, the most useful pattern is pulling prior pathology for the same patient, for example comparing a new lesion against that patient's earlier tumor when judging a possible metastasis. Similar-case retrieval also helps trainees. His 'holy grail' is an AI that pre-screens cases like a resident does for an attending pathologist: drafting a narrative diagnosis and CAP cancer protocol, and annotating slides with tumor dimensions, distance to margins, in situ versus invasive disease, lymphovascular invasion, and lymph node counts. He granted permission to quote him by name in our presentation.

Example quotes (verbatim from his email, permission granted):
"Transparency therefore is more important as you really don't want your doc blindly trusting an AI, do you?"
- "This is an AI essentially screening cases like a resident (trainee) does for an attending pathologist."
- "Usually the need is to pull prior pathology for the specific patient we are currently caring for."


Summary so far (Oct 16, 2026):
Two senior pathologists have weighed in so far, and they converge on the same gap: the models are not the bottleneck. The missing layer is validation, calibration, safety, and verifiability with a human in the loop. Dr. Parwani, a CONCH co-author, scoped what is buildable this weekend: safety and calibration of the model, plus external tools, tested with the right cases, the right controls, and blind stress tests with unknown variables. Dr. Dash added that AI output must be transparent and easy to verify, and that the most valuable retrieval is a patient's own prior pathology. 
The direction this points to: a second-look safety layer around CONCH rather than another diagnostic demo. Still to add: Dr. Feldman and Dr. Cornish (notes being extracted from recordings).
